# The lag-6 constant and the rune S

Prompted by a Quorum panel (quorumsays.com/q/H0h_Zo99eeL9, 2026-10-29): the constant 24 in the lag-6 relation c = 24 − 2c′ is the Gematria prime of S (53) reduced mod 29, and S is the only rune whose prime reduces to 24 (the 29 primes give 24 distinct residues). So both echoes carry a pointer to S: the lag-5 echo in which rune it prefers, the lag-6 relation in its constant. Also 2·15 ≡ 1 (mod 29): S is the inverse of 2, the multiplier of the lag-6 map, and 2 and −2 are both primitive roots mod 29.

Status: a post-hoc coincidence. The chance that a given constant equals one named rune's prime residue is 1/29 (p ≈ 0.034); it was noticed after S was already singled out by the lag-5 echo, and the other two facts (S = 2⁻¹, 2 a primitive root) are properties of 29 that hold whatever the data say.

Does the relation prefer the prime domain? Scanning all 812 affine maps on prime residues, p(c) ≡ a·p(c′) + b (mod 29), with exact expectations (prime residues are not a bijection) and a permutation null (199 within-page shuffles, word structure fixed):

The named form p(c) + 2p(c′) ≡ 53: 77 vs 73.9 at lag 5 (z +0.4), 52 vs 45.2 at lag 6 (z +1.0). Nothing in the prime domain; the relation lives in the index domain, where c + 2c′ ≡ 24 holds 77 times against 43.7 at lag 6 (z +5.1) and not at lag 5 (58 vs 71.5).
