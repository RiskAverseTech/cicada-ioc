# The uniform-key loophole and the swap-versus-marker test

LP2: S share 3.35 % of 12,956 runes (binomial sd 0.16 points); 11 S..S pairs among 2,073 within-word lag-5 pairs. English (Emerson, rune spelling): S share 7.43 %, S..S at lag 5 0.96 % of within-word pairs.

## (1) A key that is zero (plaintext in clear) or constant inside a fraction f of the words

With key 0 inside the gated words, cipher S..S at lag 5 there is the plaintext rate and the S share rises toward English's. With a constant non-zero key per word, S..S at lag 5 needs plaintext (S − k)..(S − k), which averages over k to no S preference, so zero is the worst case. Predictions by f:

|---|---|---|---|

| 0.05 | 3.55 % | +1.3 | 3.3 |
| 0.10 | 3.76 % | +2.6 | 4.2 |
| 0.15 | 3.96 % | +3.9 | 5.1 |
| 0.20 | 4.17 % | +5.2 | 6.0 |
| 0.30 | 4.57 % | +7.8 | 7.7 |
| 0.50 | 5.39 % | +12.9 | 11.2 |

The S share allows f ≤ 0.08 at two sd, which predicts at most 3.8 S..S pairs against 11. So the panel's correction is right (the argument needs a uniform key at the echo positions) and the loophole it opens is bounded by the S share to well under the observed count.
