# Registration: is the line-first rune plaintext under one fixed key value? (2026-10-30, before any run)

**Trigger.** §64 placed §63's anomaly: the first rune of each line is drawn from a different distribution than the page (pooled z +7.2), the same on every page, at column 0 only, and not a key that restarts at the line. One cipher reading is still open and cheap: the key takes a single fixed value at every line start (the clear, k = 0, included), so that column 0 is the plaintext's line-initial letter distribution under one shift. If so the column-0 profile matches an English letter profile shifted by some k under one of the three signs, and that k is a crib into the key stream.

**Test** (`src/line_shift.py`). Column 0 of the 55 unsolved pages (594 runes), and its two parts: lines that begin a word (206) and lines that begin mid-word (388). English profiles in rune spelling from the Emerson corpus text: word-initial runes, word-interior runes, all runes. For each sign (sub c = p + k, add c = p − k, beaufort c = k − p) and k = 0…28, the predicted column distribution is the profile shifted; the statistic is the log-likelihood gain of the observed counts under that prediction over the page's own rune distribution, maximised over the 87 (sign, k) cells. Null: 5,000 multinomial draws of the same size from the page distribution, the same maximum; p = share at or above the observed. Pairings: all 594 against the all-rune and the word-initial profiles; the 206 against the word-initial profile; the 388 against the interior profile. Controls: one synthetic column per pairing drawn from the shifted profile (sub, k = 7) at the same size, which must come back with p < 0.01 and the planted k.

**Claims.** T0: controls 4/4. T1: all 594, both profiles, best-cell p ≥ 0.01. T2: the 206 against the word-initial profile, p ≥ 0.01. T3: the 388 against the interior profile, p ≥ 0.01. **Expected: T0–T3 hold** (the over-represented runes EA, P, IA, NG are rare at English word starts under any single shift that also places T, A, S, H, W among the over-represented, and the §64 glyph pattern is the simpler account). If T1, T2 or T3 fails, the fitted (sign, k) is a crib and the next registration aligns the key stream from it before any decode is read.

**Budget.** Minutes.
