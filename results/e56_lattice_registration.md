# Registration: the E56w+n decoder on a word-lattice plaintext (2026-10-31, before any run)

**Gap.** §58's rule E56w+n (English words, one filler rune in a share of the words, a per-word gated echo at lag 5 (copy the cipher rune) and lag 6 (24 − 2c), the key spent at an echo, the soft reject-on-repeat skip at q = 0.81) is the only generative rule inside every pre-registered band. Its decoders so far were halves: §48 added the echo transition to the phase 3–4 Viterbi without fillers (null), and §55/§61 added the filler transition without echoes (null on the book's keys, the prime streams and sixteen texts, power 0.73 against the fitted filler form at 230 runes). Both halves score the plaintext rune by rune with a unigram, which is why §61 found the filler half "as good as it gets": a unigram cannot tell a filler from a real rune. §69's lattice replaces the unigram with dictionary words, and its filler arm detected 30 of 30 filler plants under an unknown running key. This lane is the whole rule in one decoder, with that plaintext model, over the known-key libraries where §61 had power 0.73. The ledgers (`results/ledger_diff.md`; pnotisdev's registry, Dukotah's LEDGER as read in §41–§54) show echo-aware or null-aware decoders only as separate halves and none with a dictionary plaintext.

**What it is not expected to add.** §48 measured that an echo touches 0–3 positions in 262 runes, so the echo transition by itself cannot change a decoder's power; its presence here is for exactness of the model, not for power. The power gain, if any, comes from the word-level plaintext under fillers. This is written down so that a "holds" on the plants is not read as the echo having mattered.

**Instrument** (`src/e56_lattice.py`). For a known keystream K the decoder is a Viterbi over the key offset j at word boundaries (the transcription's separators), one slot per word. Per slot of L cipher runes, the plaintext hypotheses are: a dictionary word of L runes (the 18,580-type dictionary of §69, `results/rune_dictionary.json`, log-frequency weighted, prior log 0.60 for "no filler"); a dictionary word of L − 1 runes with one filler at any of the L positions (prior log 0.40 − log L, the filler rune at log 1/28 since it is written to differ from its left neighbour; only for L ≥ 2); or an out-of-dictionary word at log 0.04 + L·log(1/29) (the dictionary's miss rate on Bunyan and on the solved pages). Per rune the cipher must be explained by one of: the plain transition c = comb(p, K[j]), j → j + 1, with log(1 − q) when c repeats the previous cipher rune; the skip, when comb(p, K[j]) would have repeated the previous cipher rune and c does not, at log q, j → j + 2 (a second skip allowed at log q again); the filler (no key); the echo, inside the word at cipher position t ≥ 5 with c_t = c_(t−5) and p_t = p_(t−5), or t ≥ 6 with c_t = 24 − 2c_(t−6) and p_t = p_(t−6), at log r with r = 0.5 (§47's gate), the key spent (j → j + 1) and the key rune unread; literal F, when switched on, a cipher F read as a plaintext F with no key. Hypotheses that explain every rune are enumerated by hashing the slot's decryptions under each filler, skip and echo pattern into the dictionary, so the cost per (key, page) is a few thousand lookups and the libraries run in hours, not days. The decoder returns the log score, the plaintext (−1 at fillers), the fillers and echoes placed, and the key offsets. Three signs (sub C = P + K, add C = P − K, beaufort C = K − P), literal F on and off.

**Null and acceptance.** 2,000 random keystreams per (page, F) through the same decoder; the three signs share a null by symmetry. z = (score − mean) / sd of that null. A row is a candidate at z ≥ 6, rank 1 within its library and sign, above the null maximum, and readable by eye (the decoded words written out). The libraries: the book's 7,621 keystreams under the three signs (22,863 configurations, `families.ALLSETS`, the same set as §48, §61 and §67) and the 3,000 prime-stream variants p[n+s], p[n+s]−1, p[n+s]+1 for s = 0…999 under the three signs (as §67), each with literal F on and off, over the 55 pages. The English running-key class is not rerun: §69 closed it at its detection limit, and every external text is a member of it.

**Power stage, first.** 30 plants at 262 runes: Bunyan words (held out of the dictionary; coverage 96 %) with their own word boundaries, enciphered under §58's E56w+n exactly as `mechanism_lane.simulate` writes it except that the key is a library key instead of iid (fillers at the fitted rate 0.40 of §59 rather than the 0.45 the simulator carries, gate 0.5, lag-5 copy and lag-6 affine echoes, key spent, skip q 0.81 up to four, filler and echo runes written to differ from the previous cipher rune), the key a phrase or word key from the library, signs and F cycled. Each plant is decoded over its sign's 7,621 keys plus 2,000 random keystreams by three decoders: `score.decode` plain, free-null at −2.2 (§55), and this one. Reported per decoder: rank of the true key, z, rune accuracy on real positions, and for this decoder the fillers and echoes placed against those planted.

**Claims.** E1: this decoder recovers the true key (rank 1, z ≥ 6) on at least 27 of the 30 plants. E2: its recoveries are at least the free-null decoder's on the same plants. E3: the book's 22,863 configurations × 2 F × 55 pages yield no candidate. E4: the 3,000 prime streams × 3 signs × 2 F × 55 pages yield no candidate. Controls: six plants, one per (sign, F) cell, carried through the E3 sweep as extra rows and required to come out at rank 1 and z ≥ 6 (a control that cannot reach the bar is reported as a power statement, §66's lesson). **Expected: E1 holds, E2 holds, E3 and E4 hold (null).** If E1 fails the instrument is below power, the libraries are still run and reported, and the claim about the rule's decoder is limited to the measured power.

**Coverage limits, stated now.** Known keys from two libraries only; prime offsets above 999 and pnotisdev's streams are not run (§54 and §62 ran them filler-aware with the unigram decoders); the dictionary misses 4 % of tokens and an out-of-dictionary word costs the hypothesis about 3.4 nats a rune more than a dictionary word; the echo is modelled in its spent form only (§48 ran both and found the held form costs an unaware decoder more, not less, so the spent form is the conservative one for a decoder that models it); the filler is at most one per word and uniform in position, the §59 form.

**Budget.** Plants an hour; the two libraries several hours on eight cores.

**Amendment before the power stage (2026-10-31, before any registered stage ran).** The Viterbi over the key offset keeps the best eight offset states at each word boundary (a beam, since fillers and skips spread the offset by one per word and an exact Viterbi over every reachable offset is not needed: a dictionary word is worth about ten nats over an out-of-dictionary one, and the true path is locally best on the true key). The out-of-dictionary hypothesis is given three offsets (one fewer, the same, one more key than its runes) so that a page word the dictionary lacks does not throw the key out of step. The claims, null and acceptance are unchanged.
